BUILD DRAFT — not for shipping:
Verdict preview: ship after one moderate edit. The absorption was real. The remaining gaps are mostly polish, but two issues need addressing before the executor sees the spec — neither rises to architecture-level.

HIGH — issues that could meaningfully alter executor output
RT-66. The forced choices in step 3 and step 6 lack decision criteria.
Step 3 says: "The executor must explicitly pick one of the two paths in the design doc, with rationale, before building." Then describes (a) substrate-driven and (b) feed-only.
Step 6 says: "Place /admin/funnel (#185) at its intentional new home — either as the engine-health surface inside Operations or as its own top-level 'Engine' section, per the design doc's decision in step 1."
Both are forced choices, both correctly require rationale, but neither defines the criteria for "what makes this the right pick."
The executor will default to whichever feels safer. For step 3, that's (b) feed-only — easier, no coordination with #185's evolving shape, ships faster. For step 6, that's "inside Operations" — fewer top-level sections, less invention. Both default picks are plausibly wrong without criteria to weigh against.
For step 3 specifically: the criterion is whether the substrate's outputs are mature enough by the time Today ships. If the funnel substrate has been running for 4-6 weeks and the v2 readiness review has happened, (a) is right. If the substrate just shipped and outputs aren't trustworthy yet (per #185's behavioral verification step), (b) is right and the follow-up task is real.
For step 6: the criterion is whether the funnel substrate is conceptually one of "is my data flowing" (Operations) or one of "what is the engine learning" (Engine). The data-flowing framing is about freshness and health. The learning framing is about calibration, conversion rates, and bottlenecks. The substrate does both, but its primary purpose per #185 is the latter.
Severity: high. Forced choices without criteria are deferred decisions. The executor will pick safely, not correctly.
Fix: add to step 3 — "Criterion for picking (a) vs (b): is the funnel substrate from #185 considered trustworthy at the time this redesign reaches the Today step? Trustworthiness is verified by #185's behavioral verification step (3 cycles, manual count audit). If yes, pick (a). If no, pick (b) and create the follow-up task with a defined trigger condition (substrate trustworthiness verified)."
Add to step 6 — "Criterion for placement: the funnel substrate's primary purpose is engine observability — answering 'what is the engine learning, and where are the bottlenecks.' That's a different question from 'is my data flowing.' Default to its own top-level 'Engine' section unless the design doc identifies a specific reason the engine-observability framing collapses meaningfully into operations-health framing."
RT-67. The cross-#185-#199 integration is partial. The snapshot-failure banner is addressed; four other touchpoints aren't.
The funnel substrate from #185 introduces several pieces that have natural homes in the new IA but the spec only addresses one (snapshot-failure banner → Operations health view). Other touchpoints:

Cohort identity surfacing. When an analyst investigates an opportunity, does the new Intelligence section show its cohort identity tuple, re_evaluation_count, and cohort time-window status? Or does that stay buried in /admin/funnel? An analyst's job is to investigate signals; a cohort with re_evaluation_count = 12 is a powerful signal that the analyst should see in their journey.
Prior calibration table placement. The per-lever calibration table from #185 lives at /admin/funnel today. Does it also surface anywhere in the analyst's journey (e.g., as a sidebar on a per-lever investigation page)? Should it? The spec doesn't say.
Signal lineage in the analyst journey. The signal_analyzed lower-bound from #185 means an analyst looking at a signal can ask "which levers consulted this?" The data is now persisted. Does the new IA surface this on a signal detail page?
funnel_snapshot_failures table UI placement. From RT-63 in the prior round, this table backs the recurring-failure banner UX. Does its detail view (per-failure record, acknowledge button) live in /admin/funnel only, or does it surface in Operations alongside other failure views?

These aren't bugs in #199 — they're places where the IA redesign could either deliberately exclude these touchpoints (acceptable, with explicit reasoning) or deliberately include them (more work, more value). The spec is silent, which means the executor will exclude them by gravity.
Severity: high. The IA redesign is the moment to make these decisions. Doing it later means another IA pass.
Fix: add to step 1 (audit) — "The audit must enumerate the funnel substrate's user-facing outputs (snapshot-failure banner, prior calibration table, cohort identity tuples on opps, signal lineage on signals, recurring-failure detail UI) and decide for each: (a) surfaces in the new IA at location X, or (b) stays admin-only in /admin/funnel. Silence is not an option; the design doc must state the placement decision for each output."
MEDIUM — clarity and design completeness
RT-68. The two new aggregator endpoints have no shape, caching, or error contract.
Spec says /api/today/feed "composes existing alert / opportunity / approval / job-failure (and, if step 3 picks the substrate-driven path, funnel-substrate signal) endpoints into a single feed payload." OK — but:

What does the response shape look like? An array of feed items with a kind discriminator? A grouped object with sections per source?
What happens when one underlying endpoint fails — partial response or fail-fast?
What's the cache behavior? Computed per-request? Cached for some TTL? If TTL, what's the staleness policy?
What's the tenant scoping? (Implicit answer: same as the underlying endpoints, but "implicit" failed RT-30 in #185.)

Same questions for /api/operations/health.
Severity: medium. The executor will design these and they'll work, but the design will be improvised. Improvised aggregators tend to develop incompatible shapes (Today's response array doesn't match Operations' grouped object, even though both are "compose feeds from sources") and make future maintenance painful.
Fix: add to the carve-out — "Both aggregators return a discriminated-union response: { items: Array<{ kind: string, source: string, payload: T, occurred_at: timestamp, severity: 'info' | 'warn' | 'error' }>, partial: boolean, errors: Array<{ source: string, error: string }> }. Both endpoints fail-soft: if any underlying endpoint errors, the aggregator returns successful sources in items, sets partial: true, and lists the failures in errors. Caching is per-request; no server-side TTL in v1. Tenant scoping inherits from the underlying endpoints; the aggregator must not introduce its own data access — it composes responses from authenticated calls to the existing endpoints with the user's session."
This is a small spec addition that prevents large maintenance pain.
RT-69. The "lives at /admin/funnel even if it gains a friendlier path" rule is right but the friendlier-path naming isn't decided.
If the design doc decides to put the substrate inside Operations, the friendlier path is something like /operations/engine. If it goes to its own Engine section, it might be /engine/funnel or /engine. The spec says "alongside any new friendlier path" but doesn't commit.
Severity: medium. This is a small thing that turns into bikeshedding mid-implementation if not pre-decided.
Fix: the design doc (step 1 output) must include the friendlier path's name as part of the placement decision. State this in step 6.
RT-70. The behavioral-verification gap from #185 doesn't have an analog in #199.
#185's spec required post-deploy manual count auditing on a sample tenant before declaring the funnel trustworthy. #199 doesn't include any post-deploy verification of the IA decisions — the test sweep covers regression and new-flow tests, but there's no "ship-then-validate-with-real-users" step.
For an IA redesign the analog would be: after deploy, watch one operator for 30 minutes, watch one analyst for 30 minutes, document any moments where the new IA produces confusion. This is the equivalent of #185's "is the instrumentation trustworthy" — for IA, it's "is the navigation legible."
Severity: medium. Without this, the IA ships, gets used, and small confusion accumulates without a feedback mechanism.
Fix: add a post-deploy step — "Behavioral verification: within 7 days of deploy, shadow one operator and one analyst for one work session each. Document any moments where the new IA produces hesitation, wrong-page navigation, or confusion. If any pattern emerges (3+ similar issues across users), file a follow-up task to address. This is the IA equivalent of #185's instrumentation trustworthiness check."

RT-72. "Either as the engine-health surface inside Operations or as its own top-level 'Engine' section" — the section name "Engine" is a smoke-word coin flip.
The spec offers two options for funnel placement and names the second "Engine." But the section's actual name should be informed by the design doc's user-journey analysis. Calling it "Engine" by default is acceptable but unexamined — operators may not think of the funnel substrate as "the engine"; analysts might call it "Learning" or "Calibration"; decision-makers might call it "Performance."

WHAT'S STILL MISSING
RT-74. The spec doesn't address the migration cost for users with existing bookmarks, deep links, and muscle memory.
The spec covers backwards-compatibility redirects (good — bookmarks keep working). But muscle memory ("Dashboard is on the left sidebar at position 3") doesn't redirect. Power users will be slowed for days as they relearn the nav.
Severity: low. This is the kind of UX cost that's real but doesn't affect ship/no-ship.
Fix: add to the design doc — "Communication plan for the change: a one-time in-app banner on first sign-in post-deploy explaining the IA changes and where major surfaces moved (link to old → new mapping). Banner dismissable; auto-hides after 7 days."
RT-75. No mention of role-gated nav rendering.
The IA includes an Operations section. Should non-admin users see Operations at all? The /admin/funnel admin gating is preserved (good), but the broader Operations section probably has read-only views for non-admins and write/control views for admins. The spec doesn't say what non-admins see when they look at the new sidebar.
Severity: medium-leaning-low. The current sidebar likely has this concern already and it's been handled per-page, but the redesign is a chance to make it consistent.
Fix: add to step 2 — "Sidebar items render based on user role. Each item is tagged with the minimum role required to access it; users with insufficient role see a redacted sidebar (item hidden, not shown-and-disabled). The design doc lists which items are role-gated."
