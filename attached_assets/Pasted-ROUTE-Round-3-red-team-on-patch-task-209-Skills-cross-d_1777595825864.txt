RT-93 absorption — three queries spec'd, classification rule with explicit thresholds (>50% AND ≥80%), implementation summary requirement. Reproducible.
Wait — let me check this one more carefully.
The classification rule is AND, not OR. So an artifact requires BOTH (a) >50% stale AND (b) ≥80% concentration in a single lever. What about the case where 90% of approvals are 30+ days old (clearly stale) but spread across 5 levers (no concentration)? Per the rule, that's "real working backlog." But 90%-stale with even spread is also a structural problem — it means every lever is producing approvals faster than they're being acted on. The patch would render this as a healthy backlog when it's actually a different kind of artifact (universal under-acting, not producer-side bug).
Conversely, what if the per-lever concentration is 95% in one lever (clearly suspicious) but only 30% are 30+ days old (the producer side is recent and active)? Per the rule, that's "real working backlog." But 95% concentration in one lever IS the producer-side-bug signal regardless of age distribution — a single lever firing wildly is the artifact case.
The AND threshold is conservative — it only classifies as artifact when both signals strongly agree. Real-world data is rarely that clean. The rule will under-classify artifacts.
Severity: medium. Not critical — the executor will follow the rule, and the queries themselves will surface the underlying numbers, so even if the classification is "real working backlog" the executor seeing 90%-stale or 95%-concentration will probably file the cleanup task anyway. But the classification is what determines what ships in the card, and the rule as written is overly restrictive.
Fix recommendation: change the classification rule from AND to OR — "if 30+-day-stale rows are >50% of the total OR the per-lever breakdown shows ≥80% concentration in a single leverId, classify as artifact." Either signal alone is sufficient evidence of a non-working-backlog cause. Both together is even stronger evidence, but the absence of one shouldn't disqualify the other.
This is a real finding. RT-99.
