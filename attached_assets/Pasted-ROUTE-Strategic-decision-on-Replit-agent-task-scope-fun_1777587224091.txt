THINK:
The framing is right. Here's why, and here's the trap.
Why it's right: The funnel does three things no other proposal did. It (1) makes "where is learning happening" answerable with a conversion rate at every stage, (2) prioritizes interventions by bottleneck rather than by Claude's opinion, and (3) collapses the surface/engine distinction — the surface IS the engine made visible. This is the systems-of-systems shape. Every prior proposal was a single loop or a single mechanism; this is the loop-of-loops.
The trap: "User-facing /fusion landing surface in v1" is the same mistake as #180. Decision-makers don't want to see a funnel. They want to see "what should I do today and why." The funnel is the substrate that PRODUCES that answer — but it's not the answer itself. Showing a CPO a 7-stage conversion funnel is showing them your factory floor. They want the product off the line.
There's also a measurement problem with v1-user-facing. You don't yet know which conversion rates are tight vs. loose, which drill-downs are useful vs. noise, which stage labels make sense to non-operators. Shipping that to decision-makers means committing to a UX before you know the data. You'll redesign it three times.
The 10x version: admin observability v1, then let the funnel run for 4-6 weeks with operators-only watching it. The bottleneck reveals itself. The intervention against that bottleneck ships next. THEN — informed by what actually moved — the user-facing surface is built around the one or two bottleneck-stage views that consistently produce decisions, not as a faithful render of the whole funnel. The user-facing surface becomes a curated slice, not a transparent window.
Failure modes if you go user-facing in v1:

Empty-state hell. Most stages won't have enough volume to be statistically meaningful for 30+ days. Decision-makers see a half-populated funnel and lose trust.
Premature commitment to stage definitions. If "drafts → opps" turns out to need decomposition (good drafts filtered by prior vs. weak drafts correctly killed), you've already shipped the wrong UX.
The Decision Brief problem returns. You'll feel pressure to add narrative, "so what" sentences, recommended actions — and you're back at #180, just with a funnel sidebar.

PLAN:

Confirm: funnel-as-substrate is the right frame. (Yes — with one sharpening: it's not just measurement, it's the operating substrate. Every cycle's output is a funnel snapshot. Every intervention is targeted at a specific stage. This is what makes it systems-of-systems and not just a dashboard.)
Recommend: v1 = backend + admin observability surface only.
Specify what v1 must include for the learning the agent wants:

Per-tenant, per-lever, per-cycle funnel persistence (not just live view — historical so trends are visible)
Drill-down at every stage to the specific entities that did/didn't convert
Conversion-rate change detection cycle-over-cycle (so a stage that suddenly tightens flags itself)
Annotation hooks so operators can tag a cycle with context ("regime shift," "data backfill landed," "lever logic changed") — this is how the funnel teaches you which interventions worked


Specify the v2 trigger explicitly: after 4-6 weeks of admin-only operation, identify the 1-2 stages that consistently appear as bottlenecks AND whose drill-downs produce decisions. Those become the user-facing slice. The rest stay admin.
Kill the temptation to ship user-facing v1 with "just enough" UX. There's no "just enough" — either the funnel is the product surface (then it needs full UX investment) or it's the substrate (then operators-only is correct).

What v1 must include for learning to actually compound:

Per-tenant, per-lever, per-cycle funnel snapshots persisted historically (not just live)
Drill-down at every stage to the entities that did/didn't convert
Cycle-over-cycle delta detection on conversion rates (auto-flag stages that tighten or loosen)

Everything else stays admin. No "just enough UX" compromise — either a stage earns full product treatment or it stays operator-only.
