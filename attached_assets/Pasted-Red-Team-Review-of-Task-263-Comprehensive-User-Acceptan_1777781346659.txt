Red-Team Review of Task #263 — "Comprehensive User Acceptance Test" Specification (Procuro)
Reviewer posture: Adversarial. The spec is critiqued, not the platform.
Scope: The UAT specification as written. The actual product is out of scope.
Bottom line up front: This UAT spec, in its current form, is not safe to execute as the gate before a design-partner pilot. It would generate a confident-looking "go" recommendation that is unfalsifiable by anyone outside the tester's head, would miss the failure modes a real customer hits in week one, and bakes in a structural pro-go bias. It must be revised before execution. Detailed findings, ranked risks, and concrete edits follow.

1. Executive Summary
Overall posture
The spec reads like a thoughtful engineer's run-through script, not an acceptance-test specification in the ISTQB / ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-3 sense. It enumerates what to click but rarely defines what passing looks like. ISTQB's Certified Tester — Acceptance Testing syllabus is unambiguous on this point: "Acceptance criteria for performance efficiency requirements should provide objective measures, thus avoiding subjective" judgments. This spec violates that principle dozens of times — "friendly error message", "promptly", "sensible tab order", "advances", "no SQL leaked", "green at the end" are all undefined. ISTQB
Three structural problems compound the criteria-rigor gap:

State coupling between steps is unmanaged. Steps 3 (malformed CSV), 6 (approve/reject/snooze), 9 (non-admin invite) and 11 (force a permanently-failing job) deliberately mutate persistent state, but the spec never defines a teardown contract. Step 11 is incompatible with the exit criterion "Operations and System pages are green at the end of the run."
The scope exclusions cover the failure surface most likely to embarrass the company at pilot. Mobile, load, security, and production-shape data are all out of scope, yet a design partner will hit the platform from an iPad, will paste 250k rows of dirty data, will have an IT/SecOps team probing it. The UAT is excluding what a pilot will probe.
The framing "this UAT is the gate before we tell a design-partner customer the platform is ready to pilot" creates an incentive to recommend "go". No anti-bias controls (independent tester, no-go-by-default rule, dual sign-off, devil's-advocate review) are specified.

Beneath those, the spec also lacks: tenant-isolation tests (OWASP's #1 multi-tenant risk), audit-log integrity tests (NIST SP 800-53 AU-9 / AU-10), LLM-specific failure tests (OWASP LLM Top 10 2025: prompt injection, hallucinated citations / "Misinformation", sensitive-info disclosure), accessibility tests (WCAG 2.2 / Section 508), internationalization tests (locale-specific decimal formats are central to a procurement product), and a defined defect-severity rubric tied to the go/no-go decision.

3. Findings Clustered by Axis (1–16)
Axis 1 — Coverage Gaps
The spec covers a thoughtful happy-path tour. Missing journeys/states:

External personas: customer's auditor (read-only audit-log access?), customer's CFO (Trust Center / defense-pack review only), customer's IT/SecOps (SSO/SCIM/ logs ingest?), regulator (subpoena / data-export workflow?), design-partner exec sponsor (executive dashboard view?). None of these have a journey in the spec. Recommendation: add a §5 to the report titled "External-persona spot checks" with one screen-trace each.
Adversarial personas: (a) malicious insider — Operator who wants to exfil supplier list (export size limits? rate limits? watermarking?); (b) departing employee — Org Admin who is being offboarded and tries to access during the offboarding window; (c) compromised account — credential-stuffed Operator (does a 100-second flurry of logins from 30 IPs trigger lockout / alert?). See F-024, F-043.
Concurrency: two admins editing same retry budget; two Operators approving same opportunity (F-025); two simultaneous CSV imports of overlapping data (last-write-wins or merge?).
Failure modes: external API down (F-017), partial outage (FRED up, BLS down — funnel still computable?), database failover mid-write, queue backlog past N hours, disk full.
Regulatory/compliance: GDPR subject-access-request flow, CCPA delete-my-data flow, export controls if any supplier is in a sanctioned country, data-residency for non-US tenants.
Internationalization (F-029) and accessibility (F-010) entirely absent.
Empty / boundary states: 0 suppliers, 1 supplier, 0 signals, exactly-at-pagination-boundary, 0 collectors enabled.

Axis 2 — Acceptance Criteria Rigor
The single largest defect class. Quoting the spec back at it: "verify it works", "advances", "advances", "promptly", "successfully", "non-empty", "consistent", "sensible", "rendering without errors", "no SQL leaked", "friendly", "appears", "updates", "sanitized accordingly", "in-flight HTTP fetches" (verify how?). ISTQB CT-AcT § "Specifying acceptance criteria" requires criteria be "objectively measurable, unambiguous, and testable." The spec fails this on roughly 30+ lines.
Concrete fixes are given in F-013 through F-049. The pattern: every "verify X" should rewrite to Given/When/Then with measurable assertion. Example:

Before: "verify the funnel updates"
After: Given the engine funnel snapshot at T0, when the Operator approves opportunity seed-opp-001 (dollar_value=$42,000) at T1, then at T1+5s the Approved bucket count = T0 count + 1, the Realized Savings (candidate) total increases by exactly $42,000, and the audit log contains a row with action=approve, opp_id=seed-opp-001, actor=alice@...

Axis 3 — Persona Realism
The four named personas (Operator, Analyst, Org Admin, Platform Engineer) are named, not specified. The spec never states each persona's:

Mental model (Operator thinks in alerts/queue; Analyst thinks in trend lines; Org Admin thinks in policy + auditability; Platform Engineer thinks in throughput/SLO).
Failure tolerance (Operator can tolerate a 10s page; CFO viewing Trust Center cannot tolerate a stale chart on an exec demo).
Privilege envelope (Analyst — can they download evidence? Trigger collectors? Switch Disclosure Policy? Spec implies "no" for the last but never tests it).
Tools (Org Admin uses Okta-federated SSO? MFA? IP-allowlist?).

External personas (auditor, CFO, IT/SecOps, regulator, exec sponsor) and adversarial personas (malicious insider, departing employee, compromised account) are entirely absent — see Axis 1. Without these, the UAT is literally testing the engineering team's mental model of itself.
Fix: Add a "Persona Sheet" appendix: for each persona — primary route, mental model in one sentence, top 3 success criteria, top 3 frustration triggers, privilege envelope, identity provider used, device class. Map every step to ≥1 persona.
Axis 4 — Environmental Assumptions
Per Google's hermetic-test guidance, an irreproducible environment makes every result a hypothesis, not a fact. The spec's environment is under-specified across:

Network: no proxy/MITM/strict-CSP test (F-035).
Browser: "desktop Chrome only" — version unspecified. (F-018)
Time zone / DST / NTP: unspecified, unrecorded. (F-029, F-018)
Multi-tenant isolation in dev: is the dev environment shared? Could a parallel developer's CSV upload pollute the seed? (F-005)
Secrets: how are tenant credentials loaded? Are they real? Test? Rotated post-run?
Third-party sandboxes: F-017. Rate limits on FRED (120 req/min by default), BLS (500/day for unregistered), EDGAR (10/s recommended), commercial sanctions feeds — all unmeasured. A UAT that bursts these mid-run will silently produce empty trend charts and the tester will record "Pass — chart rendered."
Browser extensions / ad blockers: a uBlock rule may break a third-party widget. F-018 captures this.

Axis 10 — Observability & Evidence
A passed test in a UAT is only valuable if it can be reproduced months later when a defect is filed. Today the only evidence is screenshots + tester narrative.

Axis 11 — Exit Criteria Ambiguity

"Operations and System pages are green" undefined. Green = exactly zero failing collectors? Zero failing-but-acknowledged? Zero in last 24h vs zero ever?

Fix: Add Step 13 — Cleanup. Define green numerically (failed_jobs_last_24h == 0 OR all_failed_jobs_have_acknowledged=true). Make cleanup an audited admin action. Include a "did cleanup succeed?" assertion as the final exit gate.
Axis 12 — Scope-Creep & Scope-Omission
The spec excludes load, security, mobile, and prod-shape verification. Each is justified individually but collectively the exclusions are exactly the surface a design partner probes:

Security/pen test: deferred to a "separate task." Has that task run? What was its scope? Does it overlap the absence of cross-tenant testing here? F-006 will be re-asked at SOC 2 audit.
Production verification: the dev environment may have weaker rate limits, looser firewalls, friendlier error pages. The pilot will not.
AI/LLM-specific (F-008): prompt injection, hallucinated citations, and model-version drift are the differentiating risk class for this product. Excluding them means the spec excludes the most likely customer-facing failure mode.

Fix: Either add minimum sentinel tests in each excluded category (one mobile pass, one 1M-row CSV, one tenant-isolation test, one prod-shape smoke) or explicitly accept the residual risk in writing, signed by the exec sponsor.

7. Honest Meta-Assessment — Should This UAT Be Run As Written?
No. Revise before execution. Do not replace.
The spec has a sound shape — a recognizable pass-through-the-product end-to-end run, organized by persona, with a written report. The bones are correct. But run as written, it would produce a confident-looking "go" recommendation built on:

Subjective acceptance criteria that two testers cannot reproducibly score (Axis 2 — pervasive).
An undefined go/no-go rule that defaults to confirmation bias under the framing pressure (Axes 5 + 13).
An exit criterion that contradicts a deliberate test action (F-001).
An evidence base of screenshots that cannot be replayed (Axis 10).
Zero coverage of the multi-tenant, audit, accessibility, i18n, and LLM-output failure classes most likely to bite at pilot (Axes 1, 8, 9, 12, 16).

A "go" produced by this UAT would not survive a customer's CISO walkthrough or a 30-minute hostile poke at the platform.
