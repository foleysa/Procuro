RT-59. The $100 dead-band is unit-blind across lever scales.
The agent added: "helping if delta_median < −$100, hurting if delta_median > +$100, neutral otherwise. The $100 dead-band prevents flipping verdicts on rounding-scale differences."
Sensible idea, but procurement levers operate at vastly different magnitudes. A small-spend lever with median realizations of $500 will flip "helping/hurting" verdicts on tiny actual differences (a $100 dead-band is 20% of typical magnitude). A large-spend lever with median realizations of $50,000 will register everything as "neutral" because $100 is rounding error (0.2%).
This isn't a bug that produces wrong code. It's a definition that produces noise on small levers and false-neutrals on large levers. The executor will ship it as written and it'll work correctly on day one — the verdict will just be miscalibrated until someone tunes it.
Severity: medium. Not a ship-blocker. The metric is correct; the dead-band is too coarse a knob.
Recommendation (not a required fix): consider replacing the absolute $100 with a relative dead-band — "neutral when |delta_median| < 5% of raw_median_abs_error, helping/hurting otherwise." This auto-scales across lever magnitudes. But this can also be discovered and tuned post-deploy when the executor sees the actual distribution. Defer to executor judgment during implementation; flag in operator README as a tunable.
RT-60. The pre-implementation validation requirement is good, but doesn't define what the executor does if the validation reveals a problem.
Spec says: "before the executor commits to the formula, they must pull a sample of historical realizations... compute the proposed median absolute error formula on the actual data, and inspect the distribution. If the executor finds the median is itself dominated by structural outliers (e.g. a single lever produces 90% of realizations and that lever has a known projection bug), document the finding in the operator README. The metric ships either way..."
"The metric ships either way" is the right call — don't let validation become a blocker. But what about cases the spec didn't anticipate? E.g., the validation reveals that 90% of realizations have realized_savings_usd = 0 (everything was cancelled), making median absolute error = raw_projected for nearly everything and the comparison trivially noise. That's not "a single lever has a bug"; it's "the data shape doesn't support the metric at all."
Severity: low. The executor will probably make a sensible call, but the spec should give them explicit permission. Recommendation: add one line — "If validation reveals the data shape cannot support the metric (e.g. realizations are predominantly zero), document this in the operator README and ship the surface with the calibration table marked 'awaiting sufficient realization variance' across all levers. This is acceptable; the funnel still earns its keep on the other nine stages."

RT-64. Snapshot writer step doesn't say where the per-lever drill-down counts come from.

Severity: low. The executor will figure this out from the stage definitions. But it's a place where ambiguity could produce subtly wrong drill-downs.
